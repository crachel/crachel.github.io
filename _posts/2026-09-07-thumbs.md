Movies had a great rating system. Then along came Letterboxd.

<!--more-->

In the old days, if you liked a movie that was a "thumbs up". Yes or no. Siskel and Ebert. Now Letterboxd lets its users rate movies on a scale of 1 to 5 stars. Maybe 0 to 5. There may also be half star ratings. I've seen hearts on there too.I don't know exactly, I don't have an account to check. I can afford one, it's not that. The accounts are probably free anyway, which means I could easily afford it. How many potential ratings is that 0 to 5 with half stars, 11?

* **0.0** stars
* **0.5** stars
* **1.0** star
* **1.5** stars
* **2.0** stars
* **2.5** stars
* **3.0** stars
* **3.5** stars
* **4.0** stars
* **4.5** stars
* **5.0** stars

The problem with this is people can't actually rate movies on a scale of 0 to 5 with half stars. I didn't type that list out, by the way. The tell is "1.0 star" correctly not being plural. I would've copy/pasted and missed that for sure. Here's a list I can type out myself:

* Thumbs **up**
* Thumbs **down**

See? I'd bet even people on that site can't explain what they rated a 2.5 star movie 4 years ago vs present day 2.5 stars. I hovered over the hearts there and it says "liked". That adds more confusion to me. I'm checking Spider Man and a bunch of people made a 4 star rating with no heart. Insanity.
